# Wingle — Brand Guidelines (Internal)

These guidelines define how Wingle should **look**, **sound**, and **behave** across marketing pages, product UI, comms, and AI-generated output.

**Scope**
- Applies to: **all products in the Wingle ecosystem** (letswingle.com, Wingle app, Wingle Pro, Wingle Atlas, winglepass.com), product marketing, emails, decks, PR, and in-product copy.
- Does not define final visual identity details (logo usage, colors, type scale). Those will live in `docs/visual-identity.md` once finalized.

**Source of truth**
- `docs/project-definition.md`
- `docs/north-star.md`
- `docs/constraints.md`
- `docs/roadmap-strategy.md`

**Non-negotiables**
- This is a **top-of-funnel company website**, not a product app. No implied login, checkout, or self-serve purchasing.
- **B2C presence is crucial**. Never imply the consumer product is deprioritized or “on hold.”
- Public naming uses **Wingle Pro** (B2B airport services), **Wingle Atlas** (B2B itineraries), and **Wingle for Travel Professionals** (B2B umbrella). Avoids internal names like “Wingle Pass” / “Wingle Chat” as standalone product names.
- **Wingle Pro positioning (important nuance)**: describe Wingle Pro as a **trusted partner / service model for travel agencies** (agencies bring the travelers; we confirm and coordinate premium airport services). Avoid framing it as “a B2B product” or “a platform” in public copy unless specifically approved.
- **Agency channel compliance (contract-critical)**: our provider contracts only allow us to sell premium airport services to the **end traveler**. Public copy must never imply that agencies *buy* or *resell* those services. The safe framing is: **agencies introduce their travelers** → Wingle confirms options and coordinates delivery for the traveler → agencies stay in control of the client relationship.

## 1. Brand Essence

Wingle is a premium travel brand that helps people and partners **go further with confidence**—from agencies delivering exceptional airport experiences, to travelers discovering a more connected, elevated journey. We sit at the intersection of **premium travel operations** (curated airport services) and **travel experience design** (itineraries and connection), translating complexity into calm, high-trust experiences.

**What Wingle stands for (in practice)**
- **Confidence to explore**: we reduce uncertainty in moments that matter (airports, journeys, transitions).
- **Premium, not precious**: elevated outcomes delivered with operational clarity.
- **Curated trust**: we don’t list everything; we apply judgment and quality standards.
- **Transformation that’s real**: we improve the travel experience through thoughtful design and dependable execution, not hype.

**Problem space**
- High-stakes travel moments where people need **certainty, speed, and care**.
- Agency workflows where premium service must be **reliable, repeatable, and easy to request**.
- Modern travel communication where experiences should be **human, connected, and memorable**.

## 2. Brand Personality

Primary archetype: **Explorer**. Secondary: **Magician**.

We are: **Dreamers, not utopians. Ingenious, not jokers. Passionate, not out of touch. Restless, not impatient. Respectful, not stiff. Classy, not classic.**

### Trait 1: Calm confidence
- **Means**: we sound certain without being loud; we set expectations plainly; we earn trust through specificity.
- **Not**: arrogant, salesy, overpromising, “too cool to explain.”

### Trait 2: Premium with warmth
- **Means**: elevated language, refined restraint, and human courtesy; we make people feel looked after.
- **Not**: stiff, elitist, cold, or exclusive-for-exclusion’s-sake.

### Trait 3: Curated and discerning
- **Means**: we highlight what meets the bar; we explain the “why” behind choices; we prefer fewer, stronger claims.
- **Not**: exhaustive catalogs, feature dumps, or “everything for everyone.”

### Trait 4: Ingenious and practical
- **Means**: smart solutions described simply; clarity beats cleverness; we remove friction.
- **Not**: gimmicky, jokey, or overly abstract.

### Trait 5: Exploratory energy (measured)
- **Means**: we invite discovery and possibility; we reference journeys and transformation when it helps understanding.
- **Not**: fantasy language, vague inspiration, or mystical claims.

## 3. Voice & Tone

### Default voice (most of the time)
- **Clear, concise, and confident**
- **Concrete**: specific nouns, verbs, and outcomes
- **Respectful**: never patronizing; no corporate fluff
- **Spain Spanish**: use **tú** (not “usted”) in ES

### Tone split by audience (critical)

#### B2B tone (Agencies, enterprise partners)
Optimize for: **premium + reliable + curated + fast** with a *touch* of Explorer/Magician.
- **Write like**: a trusted operations partner with taste.
- **Signal**: responsiveness, process, quality bar, and clarity on “what happens next.”
- **Avoid**: playful slang, hype, or anything that sounds improvised.

Examples (EN)
- Do: “Talk to our team. Tell us the route and timeline—we’ll confirm options quickly.”
- Don’t: “We’ll make your clients feel like royalty instantly.”

Examples (ES)
- Do: “Habla con nuestro equipo. Cuéntanos aeropuerto, fecha y necesidades; te respondemos rápido.”
- Don’t: “Te lo solucionamos en un momento, sin complicaciones.”

#### B2C tone (Travelers)
Explorer/Magician has more weight here, within boundaries.
- **Write like**: a journey mate—optimistic, curious, elegant.
- **Signal**: discovery, connection, and elevated experience.
- **Avoid**: cheesy motivation, influencer tone, or “life hack” energy.

Examples (EN)
- Do: “Make the journey feel lighter—before, during, and after the flight.”
- Don’t: “Unlock the secret to perfect travel.”

Examples (ES)
- Do: “Haz que el viaje se sienta más ligero—antes, durante y después del vuelo.”
- Don’t: “Descubre el truco definitivo para viajar.”

### Tone shifts by context

- **Marketing pages**: aspirational + concrete. Lead with outcomes, back them with specifics.
- **Product UI**: short, action-first. Remove adjectives unless they prevent ambiguity.
- **Errors / edge cases**: calm, helpful, never blaming. Offer next step and alternative path.
- **Technical explanations (B2B)**: plain language, structured, no jargon unless defined. Show the integration model “at a high level” without implying a developer portal.

### Explicit do / don’t rules

- **Do**: prefer verbs that imply guidance and partnership: “talk”, “explore”, “confirm”, “request”, “share”, “review”.
- **Don’t**: use certainty verbs for things that depend on availability: “guarantee”, “always”, “instant”, “zero”, “best”.
- **Do**: state what happens next: “We’ll follow up within X” (only if true) or “We’ll follow up quickly.”
- **Don’t**: imply self-serve checkout, real-time booking, or “sign up.”
- **Do (Wingle Pro)**: say “agencies bring us their travelers” / “supporting agencies with their travelers’ airport needs.”
- **Don’t (Wingle Pro)**: say “we sell to travel agencies” / “agency product” / “agency pricing” / “reseller” / “wholesale.”

## 4. Messaging Principles

### How we talk about value
- **Lead with the human outcome**: calm, confidence, premium experience.
- **Support with operational reality**: curation, responsiveness, workflow fit, clear next steps.

B2B value framing (preferred)
- “A premium standard you can trust.”
- “Curated options, confirmed quickly.”
- “Designed to fit agency workflows.”

B2C value framing (preferred)
- “A better journey: connection + premium travel experiences.”
- “Travel that feels more human.”

### How we talk about features
- Use **“what it enables” → “how it works (briefly)” → “what you do next.”**
- Prefer **fewer features** with stronger proof over long lists.
- If a detail varies (availability, pricing), say so plainly and move to the next step.

### How we talk about limitations
We are transparent without undermining confidence.
- Say what we **can** do, then what’s **variable**, then how we **handle it**.
- Never hide constraints behind vague language.

Examples
- Do: “Some services depend on airport and timing. Share your details and we’ll confirm options.”
- Don’t: “Availability may vary. Terms apply.”

### Pricing / trade-offs
This site is not transactional. Pricing language must:
- Avoid “starting at” unless ranges are stable and approved.
- Prefer “confirmed per request” when price depends on specifics.
- Never imply a public price list or checkout.

### Words/phrases to prefer
- **B2B**: curated, premium standard, confirmed, guided, responsive, seamless, reliable, high-trust, tailored.
- **B2C**: discover, connect, explore, journey, elevate, feel, moments, flight, meet.

### Words/phrases to avoid
- **Hype**: game-changing, revolutionary, best-in-class, world-class, magic (as a claim), guaranteed.
- **Aggressive luxury**: exclusive, elite-only, for the few.
- **False precision**: instant, zero contact, always available, guaranteed fast track.
- **App/checkout implications**: sign up, subscribe, buy now, checkout, add to cart.

## 5. Visual Direction (Conceptual)

Until `docs/visual-identity.md` is finalized, treat this as conceptual guidance that should hold across any design system.

### Overall feel
- **Premium, modern, calm**
- **High clarity** with tasteful restraint
- **Aspirational travel** without clichés (no generic jets/handshakes stock vibe)

### Layout, whitespace, hierarchy
- Use **generous whitespace** to signal confidence and premium restraint.
- Establish a clear reading order: headline → supporting line → proof → CTA.
- Prefer **strong typographic hierarchy** over decoration.

### Contrast and readability
- Keep contrast high enough for accessibility; never sacrifice legibility for mood.
- Use contrast to separate “narrative” from “proof” (e.g., testimonials, numbers, tiers).

### How “premium” should be expressed
Premium = **precision + restraint + craft**, not gold gradients.
- Fewer UI elements; higher-quality spacing and alignment.
- Motion (if used) is subtle, purposeful, and fast.
- Photography/illustration (if used) feels editorial and human, not overly glossy.

### How “approachable” should be expressed
Approachable = **clear language + helpful defaults + respectful guidance**.
- CTAs that explain the next step (“Talk to our team”) over pressure.
- Forms that feel calm and quick, with clear expectations.

## 6. UX & Product Feel

### How the product should feel to use
- **Fast and unblocked**: visitors should always know what to do next.
- **High-trust**: we explain just enough to feel real and credible.
- **Premium calm**: the experience is quiet, not loud.

### Speed vs. clarity trade-offs
- Prefer **clarity** when the decision is high-stakes (B2B services, contact).
- Prefer **speed** for low-stakes browsing (home, overview, exploration).
- Default rule: if removing a sentence increases ambiguity, keep it.

### How much the UI should explain vs. stay silent
- Explain:
  - What Wingle is (company + portfolio)
  - The three audience paths (agencies/partners/travelers)
  - What happens after a CTA/form submission
- Stay silent on:
  - Internal product names (“Pass”, “Chat”)
  - Internal strategy shifts (B2C vs B2B focus)

### Error handling philosophy
- Errors are **rare, calm, and recoverable**.
- Always provide:
  - What happened (brief)
  - What the user can do now
  - A fallback path (e.g., email/contact)
- Never shame users or imply fault.

## 7. Brand Boundaries (Very Important)

### What we must never sound like
- A fintech/SaaS growth site (“scale your pipeline”).
- A nightclub luxury brand (flashy, exclusionary).
- A generic travel marketplace (endless options, lowest price).
- A mystical “manifest your dream trip” coach.

### Forbidden promise patterns
Do not publish copy that implies:
- **Guaranteed availability** at any airport/time.
- **Instant confirmation** if it depends on third parties.
- **No human involvement** for premium services (we often *lean into* human coordination to build trust).
- **Self-serve booking / checkout** on `letswingle.com`.
- **“Zero contact”** or similarly absolute claims.
- **Agencies purchasing or reselling premium airport services** (agency-as-buyer framing).

### What would immediately feel off-brand
- Overly playful B2B copy, memes, slang, emojis, or sarcasm.
- Big claims without concrete explanation (“premium, elevated, best” with no substance).
- Feature lists that read like a spec sheet.
- Any phrasing that frames B2C as “secondary” or “not the focus.”

## 8. AI Usage Guidance

AI-generated output must behave like a disciplined Wingle team member: calm, premium, concrete, and respectful of constraints.

### Optimization targets (what AI should optimize for)
- **Clarity**: a reader understands the offer in one pass.
- **Trust**: copy feels credible and operationally grounded.
- **Tone fit**: B2B vs B2C tone split is respected.
- **Constraint safety**: no forbidden promises; no internal product naming.
- **Next step**: each section naturally leads to a single clear action.

### How AI-generated copy should behave
- Prefer short paragraphs, strong verbs, and concrete nouns.
- Use journey language as seasoning, not the main ingredient (especially in B2B).
- If a claim depends on context (airport, timing), phrase it as “confirmed per request.”
- Default CTA language: **“Talk to our team”** (variations allowed per page, but keep it calm).

### AI checklist (validate before shipping)
- [ ] Uses public brand architecture: **Wingle** (app), **Wingle Pro** (B2B airport services), **Wingle Atlas** (B2B itineraries), **Wingle for Travel Professionals** (B2B umbrella). No “Wingle Pass” or “Wingle Chat” as standalone product names in public copy.
- [ ] Tone matches audience: B2B = premium/clear; B2C = more exploratory.
- [ ] No forbidden promises (guarantees, instant, always, zero contact, self-serve checkout).
- [ ] Limitations are stated plainly with a next step (“share details → we confirm”).
- [ ] No mention of internal strategy shifts (never “B2C maintenance mode” or “we’re focusing on B2B”).
- [ ] CTAs guide, not pressure; default to “Talk to our team.”
- [ ] **Wingle Pro compliance**: copy frames agencies as bringing travelers; does not imply agency purchase/resale; traveler is the end client.
